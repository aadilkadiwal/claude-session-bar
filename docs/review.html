Session Bar review

I measured the installed app on your Mac, went through the code and your real session data (172 saved sessions), and compared it with five similar tools. Below: what is working, what to fix, and 14 suggestions in priority order. Each one says why it's useful and shows how it would look.

Summary

Where it stands

20 + 1unit tests passing, plus an end-to-end phone test you switch on
88 MBmemory in use (normal for a SwiftUI menu bar app)
~3.5%of one CPU core while you do nothing. Should be under 0.5%.
17near-empty saved sessions (under 40 KB) cluttering the list

Strong already

  • Session control nobody else has. Phone on/off without restarting, leftover cleanup, delete, resume in an iTerm tab. The other tools only show numbers.
  • Safe by design. Only types into idle sessions, never into a draft, deletes go to the Trash, settings are backed up.
  • No login, no server. Reads Claude Code's own files and status line, the same approach the popular trackers use.

Main gaps

  • It doesn't tell you anything. No alert when a session is waiting for you or a limit is close. You have to open the menu.
  • Percent without pace. "42%" doesn't say whether you'll run out in 20 minutes or never.
  • Busy in the background. It starts claude agents every 5 seconds even when nobody is looking.
  • Hard to tell sessions apart. Two rows called "stray keystroke", several called only by an id.

P1EFFORT M

1. Tell me when a session needs me

What
A macOS notification when a session starts waiting for you (a question or permission) or finishes a long task, plus an amber dot on the menu bar icon until you look. Clicking the notification opens that session's iTerm tab.
Why
The leftover session from 11 Sep sat "waiting for you" for three weeks unnoticed. With several sessions in different folders, the time lost is waiting on a question you didn't see. The app already detects the "waiting" state, so this mostly needs the notification part.
Note
Your notch-so-good hooks already announce these events. Pick one so you don't get double alerts: Session Bar can read the same events, or leave alerts to the notch app and only show the menu bar dot.

2. Show pace, not only percent

What
Under each ring: "At this pace you'll reach 100% at 3:40 PM" or "Safe until reset". The app saves a small history of usage numbers and works out the current rate.
Why
42% means very different things two hours before the reset and ten minutes after it. A forecast tells you when to slow down, switch to a cheaper model, or stop starting new sessions. ccusage and Usage Monitor are popular mainly because of this feature.
How
Each new status snapshot is appended to a small local file (time + percentages), using the data Session Bar already receives. Rate = change over the last 30–60 minutes.

P1EFFORT S

4. Lighter background work

What
Run claude agents every 5 seconds only while the dropdown or the details window is open. Otherwise rely on the folder watcher, which already reacts instantly to sessions starting and stopping, plus a check once a minute.
Why
Measured on your Mac: about 3.5% of one CPU core while idle (1.7% for the app, plus 1.8% for the ~17,000 claude processes it starts per day, each briefly using 150 MB). After the change it should be under 0.5%, which helps battery on a laptop. Nothing visible changes.

What

Why
Hitting the limit mid-task is the worst moment to find out. A warning at 80% leaves time to finish what you're doing or move to a lighter model. It pairs with suggestion 2.

7. Tell similar sessions apart, and rename them

What

Your list has two "stray keystroke" rows and several titled only by an id. Choosing the wrong one to resume or delete is easy when the names match.

Considered and left out

Ideas I don't recommend

Refreshing usage on a timer

The only way to get fresh numbers while idle is to send Claude a tiny message, which uses up part of your limit. Usage only changes when you use Claude anyway, and the app already drops a ring to 0% on time at a reset.

Cost in dollars

ccusage shows a cost at API prices. On a subscription plan that number isn't what you pay and tends to cause worry. Percent of your limit, plus a pace forecast, is the useful signal.
